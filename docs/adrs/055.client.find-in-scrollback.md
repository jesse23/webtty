# ADR 055: client — Find in the scrollback with libghostty-vt's search

**SPEC:** [client](../specs/client.md), [key-bindings](../specs/key-bindings.md), [config](../specs/config.md)
**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-14

---

## Context

The terminal is drawn on a canvas, so the browser's find (Cmd+F, Ctrl+F) finds nothing in it, and Cmd+F opens a find bar that searches only the page's empty DOM. There is no way to search the output.

Ghostty 1.3 has search (`src/config/Config.zig`, `Keybinds.init`, on `main` at the time of writing):

- **Bindings.** On macOS `super+f` is `start_search`, `super+shift+f` `end_search`, `super+g` `navigate_search:next` and `super+shift+g` `navigate_search:previous`; elsewhere `ctrl+shift+f` is `start_search`. On every platform `escape` is `end_search`. All are `performable:`: `end_search` and `navigate_search` with no search open are not performed, and the key goes to the app (Escape stays the app's until a search is open).
- **The bar** (`src/apprt/gtk/ui/1.2/search-overlay.blp`, `search_overlay.zig`): a "Find…" entry at the top right of the surface, a `selected/total` label (`0/0` with no matches, `0/n` before a match is selected), up and down buttons and a close button. Typing searches as you type (GTK's search entry debounces it); Enter is `navigate_search:next`, Shift+Enter `navigate_search:previous`. Starting a search when one is open focuses the entry and selects its text. Nothing is selected until the first Enter: all matches are highlighted, and the first `next` selects the newest one.
- **Order.** `next` moves toward older output, from the bottom of the screen up into the scrollback, and wraps around past the oldest match; `previous` moves toward newer output. The up button is `next`.
- **Matching** (`src/terminal/search/sliding_window.zig`): the page text is searched as one stream, so a match can span a soft wrap; ASCII letters compare case-insensitively (`std.ascii.indexOfIgnoreCase`) and everything else byte-exactly. There is no smart case and no regex.
- **Scrolling** (`src/terminal/search/terminal.zig`, `select`): if no row of the newly selected match is in the viewport, the viewport scrolls so the match's first row is the top row; otherwise it does not move.
- **Colours**: `search-background` / `search-foreground` (default `#FFE082` on black) for matches and `search-selected-background` / `search-selected-foreground` (default `#F2A57E` on black) for the selected one. The renderer gives the selection precedence over the selected match, and the selected match over other matches (`src/renderer/generic.zig`).
- **Output** does not move a scrolled viewport (`scroll-to-bottom` defaults to `keystroke, no-output`), so the selected match stays in view while a program writes. A key sent to the program (not a modifier) and a paste do scroll it to the bottom (`Surface.zig`, `keyCallback`, `completeClipboardPaste`).

The pinned libghostty-vt wasm (`05dcf887`, ADR 042) exports Ghostty's search as a C API (`include/ghostty/vt/search.h`, `ghostty_search_*`): a search bound to a terminal, given a needle, driven in bounded steps by `ghostty_search_feed` (reads the terminal) and `ghostty_search_tick` (works on what it read), with the total, the selected match and its index (0 is the newest), every match (newest to oldest, as selections), and `SELECT_NEXT` / `SELECT_PREV`, which can be told not to scroll. Results follow output, scrollback pruning, resize, reflow and screen switches. On 10,000 lines of scrollback a full search takes about 40-50 ms in about 25 steps of 2 ms; reading all 20,000 matches of a common word and converting them to screen rows takes about 3 ms (107,000 matches of `e`: about 11 ms); a feed with no change costs 0.5-2 ms.

webtty's viewport is its own (`viewportY`, lines up from the bottom, ADR 042); Ghostty's stays at the bottom. And webtty, as xterm.js does, scrolls a scrolled view to the bottom on any output.

## Decision

### Search with libghostty-vt

Find uses Ghostty's search, not a text index of webtty's own over ADR 050's selection text: the matching, the soft-wrap joining, the newest-to-oldest order and the tracking of output, pruning and resize are Ghostty's, at no cost in bundle size. Matching is Ghostty's: case-insensitive for ASCII letters, exact otherwise; no smart case.

The binding (`ghostty.ts`) adds, on the active screen, scrollback included:

- `setSearch(needle)`: creates the search on first use (with `SELECT_SCROLL = NONE`: the binding's viewport is not Ghostty's) and sets the needle; `''` frees it;
- `searchStep(budgetMs)`: feeds if the terminal changed since the last feed, then ticks and feeds until the search is caught up or the budget is spent; returns whether the results may have changed;
- `searchSelect('next' | 'previous')`, `searchState()` (total and selected index), `searchSelectedMatch()` and `searchMatches()` (every match as screen rows and columns, newest to oldest).

### The terminal drives it

`Terminal` (vendored `terminal.ts`) keeps the needle and runs the search in its render loop, before each render: a step of at most 4 ms, so a 10,000-line search completes over a dozen frames and typing never blocks. The match rows are read again when the results changed, at most once every ten times the last read took (a few frames for a 100,000-match needle while output streams; at once otherwise). The needle survives `reset` (a reconnect's new engine) and is set on the new one.

- `find(needle)`, `findNext()`, `findPrevious()`, `endFind()` and `onFindChange` (total, selected index) are the bar's API.
- **Scrolling to the selected match** is Ghostty's: if none of its rows is in view, the view jumps so its first row is the top row, clamped to the scrollback. SmoothScroll follows the jump, as it follows scrollbar drags.
- **While a match is selected and the view is scrolled up**, output does not pull the view to the bottom; the view keeps its rows in place (it moves by as many rows as the selected match did), as Ghostty's viewport does. At the bottom, the view follows output as before. With the bar closed, or nothing selected, webtty's scroll-to-bottom on output is unchanged.
- **A key or a paste sent to the program scrolls to the bottom**, Ghostty's `keystroke` half, which webtty had only through the echo: without it, typing with a match selected would leave the view up in the scrollback, the prompt and the output out of sight. Mouse reports do not scroll. Once at the bottom the view follows output again, the match still selected.
- **Output on the alternate screen scrolls to the bottom** even with a match selected: that screen has no scrollback to keep a view in, and its matches are its own, so a pager started while scrolled up is drawn in full.

### Highlights

The renderer draws every match in the rows it paints with the search colours, and the selected one with the selected colours, replacing the cell's colours as the selection does; the selection wins over both, as in Ghostty. The matches in view are found by binary search on the newest-to-oldest list, so a frame costs the same with 10 matches or 100,000. Each canvas row remembers the matches it was drawn with, and a row is repainted only when they change (moved with the rows on a scroll, ADR 043).

Four new optional theme keys, Ghostty's names in webtty's style, with Ghostty's defaults: `searchBackground` (`#FFE082`), `searchForeground` (`#000000`), `searchSelectedBackground` (`#F2A57E`), `searchSelectedForeground` (`#000000`).

### The find bar

`find-bar.ts`: a small DOM overlay at the top right of the terminal, outside the `#terminal` container, so keys typed in it never reach the terminal's key handling and focus in it is not the terminal's. A labelled text field ("Find", placeholder "Find…"), the `selected/total` count as Ghostty shows it (`0/0`, `0/n` before Enter, `k/n`) in a polite live region, previous and next buttons (↑ is next, toward older output, as in Ghostty) and a close button. Its colours come from the theme (background, foreground, `brightBlack` for the border).

- Typing searches after 150 ms (GTK's search-entry delay); Enter and Shift+Enter apply a pending needle at once and then go to the next or previous match.
- Escape, or the close button, ends the search: highlights cleared, the field's text kept for the next open, focus back on the terminal.
- Opening it when it is open focuses the field and selects its text; opening it again after a close searches the kept text again.

### Bindings

| Platform | Key | Action | When it cannot be done |
|---|---|---|---|
| macOS | Cmd+F | open / focus the bar | (always done) |
| macOS | Cmd+Shift+F | close | sent to the app (no search open) |
| macOS | Cmd+G / Cmd+Shift+G | next / previous match | sent to the app (no search open) |
| others | Ctrl+Shift+F | open / focus the bar | (always done) |
| all | Escape | close | sent to the app (no search open) |
| all, in the field | Enter / Shift+Enter | next / previous match | |

They go through `shortcutFor` and the capture listener of ADR 054, so a `keyboardBindings` entry for the combo wins, and `"chars": ""` turns one off. In the field the same keys (and Cmd+G on macOS, Escape everywhere) are handled by the field itself; `keyboardBindings` are terminal keys and do not apply there. The browser's own find is `preventDefault`ed for Cmd+F and Ctrl+Shift+F only: plain Ctrl+F stays the app's (forward a character in readline).

### Replay

Find has no side effects: nothing is sent to the PTY, the clipboard or the title. The replay on connect is searched like any output.

## Consequences

- Cmd+F (macOS) or Ctrl+Shift+F (elsewhere) finds text anywhere in the scrollback and on screen, matches spanning soft wraps included, with Ghostty's matching, order, colours and scrolling.
- Escape is the app's except while a search is open, as in Ghostty: with the bar open and the terminal focused, Escape closes the bar instead of reaching vim.
- The count can rise for a few frames after typing while a long scrollback is searched.
- On the alternate screen, the alternate screen's matches are found (as Ghostty does); the primary screen's come back when the app leaves it.
- The browser's own find bar is no longer reachable by Cmd+F from the terminal page (it found nothing there).
- Highlights in the rows beyond the scrollback limit go away as Ghostty prunes them; a selected match that is pruned is no longer selected (the count shows `0/n`).
- Four theme keys are added.

## Considered Options

- **A JS index of the text from ADR 050's formatter** (`selection_format_alloc` with `unwrap`). It would need the scrollback formatted on every change (tens of ms per 10,000 lines), its own row mapping across soft wraps and wide characters, and its own handling of pruning; Ghostty already does all of this, with the same matching Ghostty's own find bar has.
- **Smart case or regex.** Not Ghostty's; a later ADR if wanted.
- **Ghostty's `SCROLL_IF_NEEDED`.** It scrolls Ghostty's viewport, which webtty does not show (the binding keeps it at the bottom).
- **`VIEWPORT_MATCHES`.** Computed for Ghostty's viewport, which is always the bottom one here.
- **Select the newest match as you type** (browsers, VS Code). Ghostty highlights all and selects on Enter; following it keeps typing from moving the view.
- **A smooth glide to the match.** Ghostty jumps; a glide over thousands of lines would sweep through them.
- **F3 / Shift+F3** (Windows Terminal, VS Code). Not Ghostty's defaults; `keyboardBindings` cannot add webtty actions, so this would be a fixed extra binding.
- **Keep scrolling to the bottom on output while searching.** Each chunk of output would take the view away from the selected match, so Enter could not be used during a build.
