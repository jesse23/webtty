# ADR 045: client — Smooth mouse wheel scrolling

**SPEC:** [client](../specs/client.md)
**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-13

---

## Context

Pixel-smooth scrollback (ADR 040, ADR 043) follows a trackpad pixel by pixel, but a mouse wheel notch moves the whole distance at once: Chromium and Safari on macOS report a notch as one pixel delta of several lines, and Firefox as 3 lines. Scrolling with a mouse jumps between positions while the trackpad glides.

A TrackPoint is neither. Captured in Chrome on macOS, it sends fractional pixel deltas like a trackpad, but 30–200 ms apart instead of every frame: 4.000244 px steps when pushed lightly, and up to 335 px (about 20 lines) every 30–45 ms when pushed hard. Applied at once, those deltas jump.

The browser cannot say which device sent a wheel event. Easing every event would bring back the lag ADR 040 removed for trackpads.

## Decision

Glide toward a mouse wheel notch's or a sparse delta's target; leave trackpad deltas, which come every frame, immediate. In `src/client/smooth-scroll.ts`:

- **Classify the device as VS Code does** (`MouseWheelClassifier` in `scrollableElement.ts`, MIT). Each event gets a score: 1 when both axes move; otherwise 0.5, plus 0.25 for a non-integer delta, minus 0.5 when it and the previous delta are multiples of each other (a wheel's steady notches). The last 5 scores are weighted 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, … and a total of at most 0.5 is a physical wheel. Line and page deltas (`deltaMode` 1 or 2) are always a wheel.
- **Glide over 125 ms with an ease-out cubic**, VS Code's `smoothScrollDuration`. A notch during a glide adds to the glide's target and restarts the glide from the current position, so fast notches scroll as far as they ask, without stepping.
- **Glide a sparse delta at the input's speed.** A pixel delta that the classifier does not call a wheel, arriving 25 ms or more after the previous one, glides linearly over that gap plus one frame (at most 125 ms). A steady stream then scrolls at the speed it is sent at, and the extra frame keeps the glide going when the next delta is a little late. A TrackPoint's light 4.000244 px steps count as wheel notches (the classifier rounds them to integers that are multiples of each other), so they get the wheel glide.
- **A new glide starts from the latest frame of the one it replaces**, or a frame before the event when none is running. A frame's timestamp precedes the events handled in it, so a glide starting at the event would not move on its first frame; replaying the TrackPoint capture at 60 fps that way stalled every other frame.
- The glide moves the same fractional position as the trackpad, so it renders through the overscan canvas (ADR 043) and only copies rows when it crosses lines.
- A trackpad delta finishes the glide at its target and applies there. Output snapping to the bottom, keyboard scrolling, a scrollbar drag or a resize stops the glide where ghostty-web put the viewport.
- **Pace the ticks sent to apps.** Mouse-tracking apps and the alternate screen still get whole-line ticks (SGR scroll buttons, ADR 040, or arrow keys), which they scroll themselves, and the tick counts are unchanged. But a sparse delta's ticks no longer go out in one burst: `TickPacer` in the vendored terminal sends them evenly over the same gap plus a frame, a few per frame, starting from its latest frame like the glide. `SmoothScroll.tickSpread` makes the call with the same classifier, so wheel notches and trackpad deltas still send their ticks at once, and a zero-spread event flushes any queued ticks first. A direction change drops the queued ticks.

## Consequences

- A mouse wheel scrolls scrollback smoothly, reaching each notch's target 125 ms later.
- A TrackPoint scrolls smoothly at its own speed, about one event interval plus a frame behind the input. Replaying the capture at 60 fps moves the view on every frame, where applying deltas at once moved it by up to 335 px on one frame and not at all on the next.
- In an app (pi, Claude Code's fullscreen mode, vim), a TrackPoint scrolls a few lines every frame instead of about 20 lines every other frame: replaying the capture, a hard push went from 0 or 17–20 ticks per frame to 8–13 on every frame. An app can only move by whole lines, so this is as smooth as it gets there. Neither pi nor Claude Code has a sub-line scroll mode, and no escape sequence for one exists.
- The first delta of a trackpad gesture comes after a pause, so it glides; the next delta, a frame later, finishes that glide.
- The classifier needs a couple of events to settle; until then a notch after trackpad scrolling gets the sparse glide instead of the wheel glide. A trackpad flick with integer deltas that happen to be multiples of each other may glide for 125 ms.
- VS Code multiplies Chromium deltas by the page zoom before classifying; webtty does not, so at a browser zoom other than 100% a Chromium wheel's deltas may not be integers, and its notches get the sparse glide instead of the wheel glide.

## Considered Options

- **Ease every wheel event (xterm.js `smoothScrollDuration`).** Simple, but trackpad scrolling would trail the fingers again.
- **Classify by `wheelDeltaY` multiples of 120.** Non-standard, and what it reports for a notch varies by browser and OS; VS Code's classifier is proven across Chromium, Safari and Firefox.
