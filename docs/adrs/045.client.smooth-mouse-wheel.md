# ADR 045: client — Smooth mouse wheel scrolling

**SPEC:** [client](../specs/client.md)
**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-13

---

## Context

Pixel-smooth scrollback (ADR 040, ADR 043) follows a trackpad pixel by pixel, but a mouse wheel notch moves the whole distance at once: Chromium and Safari on macOS report a notch as one pixel delta of several lines, and Firefox as 3 lines. Scrolling with a mouse jumps between positions while the trackpad glides.

A TrackPoint is neither. Captured in Chrome on macOS, it sends fractional pixel deltas like a trackpad, but 30–200 ms apart instead of every frame: 4.000244 px steps when pushed lightly, and up to 335 px (about 20 lines) every 30–45 ms when pushed hard. Applied at once, those deltas jump.

The browser cannot say which device sent a wheel event. Easing every event would bring back the lag ADR 040 removed for trackpads.

## Decision

Glide toward a mouse wheel notch's or a sparse delta's target; leave trackpad deltas, which come every frame, immediate. The code is in four parts:

- `src/client/wheel-stream.ts`: `WheelStream` classifies each event once and picks its `Pace`, for the scrollback (`forScrollback`) and for apps (`forApp`).
- `src/client/smooth-scroll.ts`: `SmoothScroll` moves the scrollback position at that pace.
- `src/client/vendor/ghostty-web/wheel.ts`: what a wheel event sends to an app, and `TickPacer`, which paces it.
- `src/client/vendor/ghostty-web/glide.ts`: `Glide`, the one motion both follow (timed glide or spring).

In detail:

- **Classify the device as VS Code does** (`MouseWheelClassifier` in `scrollableElement.ts`, MIT). Each event gets a score: 1 when both axes move; otherwise 0.5, plus 0.25 for a non-integer delta, minus 0.5 when it and the previous delta are multiples of each other (a wheel's steady notches). The last 5 scores are weighted 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, … and a total of at most 0.5 is a physical wheel. Line and page deltas (`deltaMode` 1 or 2) are always a wheel.
- **Glide over 125 ms with an ease-out cubic**, VS Code's `smoothScrollDuration`. A notch during a glide adds to the glide's target and restarts the glide from the current position, so fast notches scroll as far as they ask, without stepping. A notch within 200 ms of one in the same direction continues linearly over its gap plus a frame instead of easing out again, so a spinning wheel, or a TrackPoint's slow 4 px steps every 150 ms, scroll steadily instead of pulsing.
- **Glide a sparse delta at the input's speed.** A pixel delta that the classifier does not call a wheel, arriving 25 ms or more after the previous one, glides linearly over that gap plus one frame (at most 125 ms). A steady stream then scrolls at the speed it is sent at, and the extra frame keeps the glide going when the next delta is a little late. A TrackPoint's light 4.000244 px steps count as wheel notches (the classifier rounds them to integers that are multiples of each other), so they get the wheel glide. A stream stays sparse until two short gaps in a row: the capture has a 25 ms gap that replays as 24.9 ms, and treating that one delta as a trackpad's snapped the scrollback 50 lines in one frame and flushed 33 ticks to an app in one frame.
- **Follow a spring while a sparse stream is fast.** When a stream's deltas come every 60 ms or faster on average (a running average of its gaps, the pause before it counting as 125 ms), the scrollback follows a critically damped spring (ω = 28 rad/s) toward the target instead of gliding linearly over each gap, and the spring takes over at the current speed. A hard TrackPoint push sends a delta every 29–45 ms, and gliding over each gap swung the speed with them. Replaying the capture through the code, the frames of a hard push went from 93–223 px to a steady 156–166 px (summed frame-to-frame change 833 → 451 px), for about 35 ms more lag; slower streams keep the linear glide, which a spring would make pulse between deltas 120 ms apart. The exact spring step (`springStep`) is stable at any frame time and does not overshoot. Once on the spring, a stream stays on it until it pauses for 125 ms or reverses: leaving it on two short gaps in a row applied the spring's pending distance at once (985 px in one frame in a replay with 22 ms gaps), and leaving it as the gaps grew at the end of a push sped the view up and then stopped it dead. Once no event has come for 60 ms, the spring's stiffness doubles, so the view stops in about 200 ms instead of coasting for 350 ms; it is settled within half a device pixel. End to end, a hard push's frames went from a frame-to-frame standard deviation of 24–43 px to 10.5–15 px, with 13 → 55 ms of lag.
- **A new glide starts from the latest frame of the one it replaces**, or a frame before the event when none is running. A frame's timestamp precedes the events handled in it, so a glide starting at the event would not move on its first frame; replaying the TrackPoint capture at 60 fps that way stalled every other frame.
- **The glide advances inside ghostty-web's render loop**, just before it renders (`attachBeforeRender`), so a frame shows that frame's position. With its own `requestAnimationFrame`, the glide ran after the render loop and every frame showed the previous frame's position.
- **The glide ignores its own line moves.** Rounding the offset to device pixels can put the viewport on the next line while the position is just below it; ghostty-web's `onScroll` then reported a line other than `floor(position)`, and the glide took it for an outside move and stopped, losing the rest of its distance (a 100 px notch stopped at 75 px in the end-to-end test).
- The glide moves the same fractional position as the trackpad, so it renders through the overscan canvas (ADR 043) and only copies rows when it crosses lines.
- A trackpad delta finishes the glide at its target and applies there. Output snapping to the bottom, keyboard scrolling, a scrollbar drag or a resize stops the glide where ghostty-web put the viewport.
- **Pace the ticks sent to apps.** Mouse-tracking apps and the alternate screen still get whole-line ticks (SGR scroll buttons, ADR 040, or arrow keys), which they scroll themselves, and the tick counts are unchanged. But they no longer go out in one burst, as the scrollback glides: `TickPacer` sends a wheel notch's ticks evenly over 125 ms and a sparse delta's over its gap plus a frame, or follows the same spring while the stream is fast (ticks per frame of a hard push went from 4–13 to 9–10), a few per frame, starting from its latest frame like the glide. The ticks go out evenly whatever the pace's easing, as an app moves in whole lines, and the count is rounded up, so the first tick goes out on the next frame and a one-tick notch is not held back. `WheelStream.forApp` makes the call with the same classifier; trackpad deltas, which already come every frame, still send their ticks at once, and a zero-spread event flushes any queued ticks first. A direction change drops the queued ticks.
- **Count arrow keys by distance.** On the alternate screen without mouse tracking (`less`, `man`), the vendored terminal sent `round(deltaY / 33)` arrow keys per event, at most 5, with no remainder kept: slow trackpad deltas and Firefox's 3-line notches sent nothing, and a hard TrackPoint push was capped at 5 lines. `wheelArrows` now sends one per line of travel (pixel deltas by cell height, line deltas by line, page deltas by rows), keeping the remainder like `wheelTicks`. With application cursor keys (DECCKM), which `less` sets, they are `ESC O A`/`ESC O B`; `less` ignored `ESC [ A`/`ESC [ B`, so the wheel did nothing in it.
- **Respect reduced motion.** With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, wheel and sparse deltas scroll the scrollback at once instead of gliding, and a wheel notch's ticks go to apps at once. A sparse stream's ticks stay paced: pacing times input, it does not animate, and sending them at once brought back bursts of 20 ticks every other frame.

## Consequences

- A mouse wheel scrolls scrollback smoothly, reaching each notch's target 125 ms later.
- A TrackPoint scrolls smoothly at its own speed, about one event interval plus a frame behind the input. Replaying the capture at 60 fps moves the view on every frame, where applying deltas at once moved it by up to 335 px on one frame and not at all on the next.
- In an app, a mouse wheel notch of several lines scrolls a line at a time over 125 ms instead of all at once.
- In an app (pi, Claude Code's fullscreen mode, vim), a TrackPoint scrolls a few lines every frame instead of about 20 lines every other frame: replaying the capture, a hard push went from 0 or 17–20 ticks per frame to 8–13 on every frame. An app can only move by whole lines, so this is as smooth as it gets there. Neither pi nor Claude Code has a sub-line scroll mode, and no escape sequence for one exists.
- The first deltas of a trackpad gesture come after a pause, so they glide over a frame or two; the second short gap in a row ends that.
- End-to-end in headless Chrome (wheel notches, a 16 ms trackpad stream and the replayed TrackPoint capture; shell scrollback, a mouse-tracking app and an alternate-screen app): tick and arrow totals match one per cell height of travel, trackpad scrolling paints in the frame its event arrives, and reversals, output while gliding and the ends of scrollback behave.
- The classifier needs a couple of events to settle; until then a notch after trackpad scrolling gets the sparse glide instead of the wheel glide. A trackpad flick with integer deltas that happen to be multiples of each other may glide for 125 ms.
- VS Code multiplies Chromium deltas by the page zoom before classifying; webtty does not, so at a browser zoom other than 100% a Chromium wheel's deltas may not be integers, and its notches get the sparse glide instead of the wheel glide.

## Considered Options

- **A spring for every sparse stream.** Evens out fast streams, but makes slow ones (deltas 120 ms apart) pulse: the spring settles between deltas (summed frame-to-frame change 16 → 80–200 px in simulation).
- **A spring whose stiffness follows the stream's gap.** Smooth at every speed, but slow streams lagged by 230 ms.
- **A steady speed from a running average of the input's rate.** It fell behind and then had to drain the backlog, which swung the speed more than the linear glide.

- **Ease every wheel event (xterm.js `smoothScrollDuration`).** Simple, but trackpad scrolling would trail the fingers again.
- **Classify by `wheelDeltaY` multiples of 120.** Non-standard, and what it reports for a notch varies by browser and OS; VS Code's classifier is proven across Chromium, Safari and Firefox.
