# ADR 045: client — Smooth mouse wheel scrolling

**SPEC:** [client](../specs/client.md)
**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-13

---

## Context

Pixel-smooth scrollback (ADR 040, ADR 043) follows a trackpad pixel by pixel, but a mouse wheel notch moves the whole distance at once: Chromium and Safari on macOS report a notch as one pixel delta of several lines, and Firefox as 3 lines. Scrolling with a mouse jumps between positions while the trackpad glides.

The browser cannot say which device sent a wheel event. Easing every event would bring back the lag ADR 040 removed for trackpads.

## Decision

Glide toward a mouse wheel notch's target; leave trackpad deltas immediate. In `src/client/smooth-scroll.ts`:

- **Classify the device as VS Code does** (`MouseWheelClassifier` in `scrollableElement.ts`, MIT). Each event gets a score: 1 when both axes move; otherwise 0.5, plus 0.25 for a non-integer delta, minus 0.5 when it and the previous delta are multiples of each other (a wheel's steady notches). The last 5 scores are weighted 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, … and a total of at most 0.5 is a physical wheel. Line and page deltas (`deltaMode` 1 or 2) are always a wheel.
- **Glide over 125 ms with an ease-out cubic**, VS Code's `smoothScrollDuration`. A notch during a glide adds to the glide's target and restarts the glide from the current position, so fast notches scroll as far as they ask, without stepping.
- The glide moves the same fractional position as the trackpad, so it renders through the overscan canvas (ADR 043) and only copies rows when it crosses lines.
- A trackpad delta, output snapping to the bottom, keyboard scrolling, a scrollbar drag or a resize stops the glide where ghostty-web put the viewport.
- Mouse-tracking apps and the alternate screen are unchanged: they get whole-line SGR ticks (ADR 040), which they scroll themselves.

## Consequences

- A mouse wheel scrolls scrollback smoothly, reaching each notch's target 125 ms later.
- The classifier needs a couple of events to settle. The first notch after trackpad scrolling may jump, and a trackpad flick with integer deltas that happen to be multiples of each other may glide for 125 ms.
- VS Code multiplies Chromium deltas by the page zoom before classifying; webtty does not, so at a browser zoom other than 100% a Chromium wheel's deltas may not be integers and the wheel scrolls as before (immediately).

## Considered Options

- **Ease every wheel event (xterm.js `smoothScrollDuration`).** Simple, but trackpad scrolling would trail the fingers again.
- **Classify by `wheelDeltaY` multiples of 120.** Non-standard, and what it reports for a notch varies by browser and OS; VS Code's classifier is proven across Chromium, Safari and Firefox.
