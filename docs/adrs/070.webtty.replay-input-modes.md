# ADR 070: webtty — Restore the input modes ahead of the scrollback replay

**SPEC:** [webtty](../specs/webtty.md)
**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-15

---

## Context

A client that joins a running session (a reloaded or second tab, or one that fell behind and reconnected, [ADR 048](048.webtty.output-flow-control.md)) starts a fresh terminal and is sent the scrollback: the last `config.scrollback` characters of output (256 Ki by default, [ADR 007](007.webtty.session-client.md)). Its terminal state is whatever that replay sets.

A full-screen app sets its input modes once, at start. herdr writes `ESC[?2004h` (bracketed paste) when its client attaches and then redraws its whole screen, about 35 KB at a time ([ADR 069](069.client.scroll-ticks-wait-for-the-app.md)), so the sequence leaves the ring after a few redraws. The new tab's terminal then has bracketed paste off while herdr and the agent inside it (pi) still expect it on. A paste goes out raw, each newline as `\r`, and pi submits the first line and queues every other line as a separate message.

Reproduced with a shell that sets mode 2004, writes 400 KB and then records its input: after a reload, 1.x's replay starts with `xxxx…` and a paste of three lines arrives as `line one^Mline two^Mline three`. Mouse reporting (1000–1006), focus reporting (1004) and application cursor keys (1) are lost the same way.

## Decision

- **The session follows the input modes through its output.** `sessions/input-modes.ts` (`InputModes`) scans each PTY chunk for DECSET/DECRST (`ESC[?…h`, `ESC[?…l`) and records the modes in `INPUT_MODES`, the DEC private modes that change what the client sends: 1, 9, 1000, 1002, 1003, 1004, 1005, 1006, 1007, 1015, 1016, 2004, 2031 and 2048. RIS (`ESC c`) clears them all; DECSTR (`ESC[!p`) clears mode 1. A sequence split across chunks is carried (at most 64 characters) to the next one. A chunk with no ESC returns at once.
- **A joining client is sent one DECSET for the modes that are on, then the scrollback**, in the same message. The replay that follows can only set or reset modes in the order the app did, so the client ends in the app's current state either way. With no mode on, the replay is unchanged.
- **Display modes are left to the replay.** Cursor visibility, the alternate screen and the like change what the tab shows, not what it sends, and an app restores them with its next redraw.

## Considered Options

- **Send the restore after the scrollback.** Rejected: the ring is cut at a chunk boundary and can end inside a sequence, which the appended DECSET would then corrupt.
- **Serialize the full terminal state on the server** (a server-side VT emulator). Rejected for this fix: it is the complete answer for a reconnect but a large change to the server's hot path; this ADR covers the modes whose loss breaks input.
- **A larger scrollback.** Rejected: it only postpones the loss, and a redrawing app fills any size.
- **Ask the app to resend its modes** (detach and reattach herdr after each reload). The workaround, not a fix.

## Consequences

- A reloaded or reconnected tab pastes into herdr and pi as a bracketed paste, and keeps mouse and focus reporting. Checked end to end with headless Chrome against the reproduction above: `ESC[200~line one\nline two\nline three ESC[201~`.
- The per-chunk path gains a `String.includes` and, for a chunk with ESC, one regex scan.
- The kitty keyboard protocol's flags (`CSI > … u`) are a per-screen stack and are not followed; an app that pushes them is in the same state as before this change after a reconnect.
