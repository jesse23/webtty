# ADR 069: client — Scroll ticks wait for the app's answer

**SPEC:** [client](../specs/client.md)
**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-15

---

## Context

Scrolling Claude Code's fullscreen mode inside herdr lagged and then jumped a long way, while Claude Code on its own, pi on its own and pi inside herdr scrolled smoothly. With a TrackPoint, Claude Code inside herdr moved continuously but in uneven jumps. 1.15 lagged the same way. Measured on a real herdr session (several Claude Code panes, a 204 × 109 pane at Retina scale):

- **webtty was idle.** A Chrome performance trace of the lag showed the page's main thread busy 5% of the time, one frame over 16 ms in 12 s, and stretches of 2.5 s in which 43 scroll ticks went out and no output came back.
- **The server passed everything on at once.** A trace of the server's input and output (1,186 ticks in about 25 s) found output flow control ([ADR 048](048.webtty.output-flow-control.md)) never blocked, at most 78 KB unacknowledged against its 2 MB limit, and every PTY chunk sent as it came. From a tick reaching the PTY to herdr's next output: 104 ms at the median, 1.0 s at the 90th percentile, 3.7 s at worst.
- **herdr redraws its whole screen for every tick.** Each tick came back as one synchronized update (DEC mode 2026) of about 35 KB, starting 30–45 ms after the tick and arriving in about 35 chunks over 8 ms, however little of the screen changed. Claude Code renders its pane, then herdr its screen.
- **Paced ticks doubled the work.** [ADR 045](045.client.smooth-mouse-wheel.md) spreads an event's ticks over frames, about one a frame, so each reached herdr and Claude Code alone and each was redrawn. Replaying a fast scroll (about 45 ticks a second) on that session at its size, paced ticks brought back 967–1,182 KB of redraws against 490–568 KB for the same ticks sent at once. Where the app needs 100 ms or more a redraw, ticks a frame apart queue up in it, and when it catches up it applies them together: the jump.
- **A TrackPoint is far faster than its old capture.** A capture on the machine in question: light pushes of 4 px every 130–350 ms, hard ones of 300–370 px every 15–60 ms, the hardest 1,150 px every 30 ms, about 2,700 lines a second. Inside herdr, Claude Code shows about a dozen updates a second, so a hard push has to move 24 lines an update.

No terminal setting changes herdr's frame time; herdr has none.

## Decision

- **Ticks wait for the app's answer.** `TickPacer` (`wheel.ts`) remembers when ticks last went out. Until the app answers, it sends no more: the ticks that come due meanwhile, from the pace's glide or spring or at once, are sent together when it does. An app that answers within a frame (pi, vim, Claude Code on its own) gets its ticks as before.
- **The answer is output that leaves no synchronized update open.** The write pipeline's `wroteOutput` tells the wheel controller (`WheelController.answered`) after each write, unless the engine still has mode 2026 on (`SyncHold.inUpdate`). herdr's frame is a single update in 35 chunks, and the next ticks wait for its end rather than its first chunk. Output for other reasons (another pane, a spinner) also counts, which at worst lets ticks go a little early, as before this change.
- **An app that does not answer gets the next ticks after `ANSWER_WAIT_MS` (100 ms)**, so an app that redraws nothing for a tick (at its top already) is not held.
- **A slow app gets at most `SLOW_GO_TICKS` (12) ticks a go.** The pacer keeps a running average of the time the app takes to answer (at most `ANSWER_WAIT_MS` a sample). While it is over `SLOW_ANSWER_MS` (40 ms), a go sends 12 ticks at most, keeps one more go queued and drops the rest, so updates move evenly and the scroll stops when the push does. A fast app is never capped.
- The arrow keys sent to an alternate-screen app without mouse tracking ([ADR 045](045.client.smooth-mouse-wheel.md)) go through the same pacer and wait the same way.

## Considered Options

- **Send ticks at once, as 1.15 did.** Rejected: it lagged the same way, and pi and vim lose ADR 045's even pace.
- **Answer on the first output after ticks.** The first version. Equal in every measure here, but it let ticks reach herdr in the middle of its frame; the end of the update is when the app has finished.
- **Throttle on the server (hold PTY input until output).** Rejected: the server cannot tell scroll ticks from typing, and the client already knows its pace.
- **Even out the go sizes without a cap.** Rejected: a hard push inside herdr still moved about 24 lines an update, only more steadily.
- **Cap every app.** Rejected: replaying the TrackPoint capture with an app answering in 10–25 ms, a 12-tick cap cut a hard push to 59% of its distance where nothing was wrong.
- **Slow the TrackPoint down everywhere.** Rejected: it scrolls well in pi and Claude Code on their own.

## Consequences

- On the herdr session above at its size, replaying the fast scroll: 31–42 ms from tick to output at the 90th percentile and 45–48 ms at worst, against 50–125 ms and 67–212 ms with paced ticks, and 48–79 ms and 49–116 ms for 1.15.
- Replaying the TrackPoint capture through the wheel code against an app answering in 35–100 ms (Claude Code inside herdr): every update during the hard push moves 12 lines, against a median of 24 and up to 190 before, and the push scrolls 28% as far. Against an app answering in 10–25 ms, nothing changes: the same 125 updates and 1,388 lines. A touchpad capture (1–5 px every 16 ms) never reaches the cap and is unchanged.
- Claude Code inside herdr still follows a scroll at herdr's pace, about one update every 30–45 ms here, slower than on its own: herdr redraws its whole screen for each tick, which only herdr can change.
- An app that answers slowly for other reasons, such as one over a slow link, is capped too while its average stays over 40 ms.
- Scroll benchmark ([SPEC](../specs/scroll-benchmark.md)), 2026-10-15, headless Chrome, the build before this change (before) against this one (after), medians of two runs with a trackpad, a wheel and a TrackPoint, plus a touchpad capture (1–5 px every 16 ms) on the fullscreen and herdr scenarios:
  - **Unchanged:** pi fullscreen and classic, Claude Code classic, vim, nvim, yazi, the shell (`seq`), the stream test and pi inside herdr, with every input; the touchpad capture everywhere; no blinks.
  - **Claude Code inside herdr:** with a wheel, 28.5 → 35.9 paints a second and 50 → 33 ms between paints at the 90th percentile; with a TrackPoint, 6.2 → 9.7 and 375 → 83 ms. Headless herdr answered within 40 ms, so the cap did not apply.
  - **Claude Code on its own and `less`, with a wheel:** 46 → 36 and 40 → 35 paints a second. Ticks the app has not answered yet go in pairs, so it redraws less often for the same distance (Claude Code: 25 → 33 ms at the 90th percentile, 73 → 65 px a paint).
  - **Claude Code on its own with a TrackPoint:** 158 → 67 ms between paints at the 90th percentile; one blink in one of two runs (0 before), as in [ADR 068](068.scroll-benchmark.app-modes-and-scroll-behaviour.md)'s runs.
